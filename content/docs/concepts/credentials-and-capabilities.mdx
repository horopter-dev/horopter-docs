title: Credentials and Capabilities
description: What a credential grants, how it is scoped to a tenant, and what Horopter leaves to you.

Every route on Horopter's two daemons, except the health probes, needs a credential. This
page explains what a credential is, what each role may do, and which protections are yours to
provide. It describes Horopter <HoropterVersion/>.

## Credentials

A **credential** is a key a producer or reader presents as a bearer token:
`Authorization: Bearer horo_<prefix>_<secret>`. Horopter stores only a hash of the secret, so a
key is shown once, when it is issued, and cannot be recovered afterwards. The prefix names the
key in logs and is what you give to revoke it.

A credential carries, besides its role:

- a **tenant**, or none: see [Tenants](#tenants);
- a **source**, the name of the system it was issued for, such as `github`;
- a **locus**, the first part of every ref it writes, which defaults to the source: see
  [What Is Horopter](/concepts/what-is-horopter#locus);
- optionally a **subject pattern**, which limits the subjects its events may name.

Credentials are issued, listed and revoked with `horoctl`, directly against Horopter's
database; see [Issue Credentials](/how-to/issue-credentials). A revoked credential is refused by
new requests within 30 seconds, the time a daemon caches a credential it has checked.

## Roles and Capabilities

A credential is issued as a **role**, and a role is a stored set of **capabilities**. A route
needs one capability:

| capability | lets a credential |
| --- | --- |
| `append` | post events to ingress |
| `read` | read changes and events from egress, through its own API and its adapters |
| `admin` | repair a change, by re-folding it from its events |
| `issue` | reserved for issuing credentials over the API, which Horopter does not have yet |

Four roles are seeded:

| role | capabilities | for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `producer` | `append` | a system that records changes, such as a CI job |
| `reader` | `read` | a tool that shows changes, such as Grafana |
| `tenant_admin` | `read`, `admin` | an operator of one tenant |
| `superuser` | `append`, `read`, `admin`, `issue` | an operator of every tenant |

Give each producer and each reader its own credential with the narrowest role that does its
job. A producer key that leaks lets its holder post events; a reader key lets its holder read
a tenant's changes. Only `horoctl`, with access to the database, issues credentials.

A subject pattern narrows `append` only: an event naming a subject outside the pattern is
refused. Reading and repairing changes are whole-tenant, so `horoctl` refuses a subject pattern
on any role that grants more than `append`.

## Tenants

A **tenant** is a partition of Horopter's data: one tenant's events and changes are never
served to another's credentials. A credential is scoped to one tenant, or to none:

- A **tenant-scoped** credential acts in its own tenant only. A producer's events land in its
  credential's tenant, and an event that names a tenant is refused. A reader may name its own
  tenant with `?tenant=` and no other.
- A **global** credential has no tenant, and names the tenant it reads with `?tenant=` on every
  request.

## Refusals

| answer | when |
| --- | --- |
| `401` | no credential, or one that is malformed, unknown, revoked or expired |
| `403` | a valid credential without the route's capability, or naming another tenant |

Every `401` has the same body, whatever the reason. Telling a revoked key from an unknown one
would let a caller probe for valid prefixes.

A read that fails inside Horopter is answered `500` with a generic body. The underlying error,
which can name tables and the database host, goes to the daemon's log instead.

## What Horopter Leaves to You

- **The database is the root of trust.** Whoever can reach Postgres can read and write the
  event log and the credential table, past every check the daemons make. `horoctl` needs no key
  of its own for the same reason: its authority is database access. Keep the database
  reachable only by Horopter and its operators.
- **Floods of requests.** Nothing in Horopter limits how many requests arrive; see
  [Read Admission](/concepts/read-admission#what-it-does-not-do). Put a WAF or equivalent in
  front of both daemons.
- **The metrics listener is unauthenticated.** It binds `localhost` by default. Bound to another
  interface, it shows metric names, route paths and your `OTEL_RESOURCE_ATTRIBUTES` to anyone
  who reaches it, though no tenant, event or database identity.
- **The health probes are open.** `/healthz` and `/readyz` need no credential, so an
  orchestrator can probe them. Point a liveness probe at `/healthz` and a readiness probe at
  `/readyz`: the images have no shell, and carry no `HEALTHCHECK`.
- **Development stacks are for one machine.** The compose file a release ships issues a
  producer and a reader key that everyone knows, and never a `superuser`. Do not run it
  anywhere its ports are reachable from another machine, or reuse its keys.
